What did the data say?

Service Level 2 (SL2) is where authors pick a service. If it is unclear, they leave. We were also introducing Post-AI Editing as a new addition to our existing service portfolio.

Before designing anything, I looked at 30 days of visitor data from Microsoft Clarity. Five things stood out.

0%

Not one of the 101 people who got stuck on the table was just browsing. Most (65) were highly interested buyers.

So what: losing those buyers is a direct loss.

User intent, dead-click sessions only (101 sessions)

92%

9 out of 10 visits happen on a desktop, not a phone. So the redesign is built for desktop first.

Devices, all sessions

66%

Most visitors stop scrolling halfway down the page. They never even see the plans.

So what: use the top fold efficiently.

Scroll depth, all sessions

36%

More than a third of people's attention stays near the very top of the page.

Attention heatmap, all sessions

91%

9 in 10 of the top traffic sources are Wiley's own pages, not a search engine.

So what: users already know what they want before they land here. Don't spend much time convincing them. Start selling.

Top referrers, all sessions

So what was going wrong?

The old SL2 listed Standard, Advanced and Premium editing in one long table. It worked for three services. It would not work for a fourth.

A 4th service, Post-AI Editing, isn't directly comparable to the others. It doesn't fit the same format.
  • 01

    Services are hard to scan and compare.

  • 02

    A 4th service, Post-AI Editing, isn't directly comparable to the others - it doesn't fit the same format.

  • 03

    The tabular format is very text heavy.

Design Decisions I made..

Goal: let users get what they want without confusing them.
01

Show only necessary information in front (Progressive Disclosure)

02

Surface prices upfront, reducing one click and possible dropoffs due to frustration.

03

Refine copy to make it easy to understand for users.

04

Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG) followed
